          Requirements for an Excavation and Lateral Support Plan
                 Building (Administration) Regulation 8(1)(bc)

Introduction

            Bulk excavation, even  if shallow, can be dangerous  if not properly
designed. Authorized persons, registered structural engineers (RSE) and registered
geotechnical engineers (RGE) are reminded to take adequate precautions to ensure public
safety whenever excavation is found necessary as part of the work. Reference should be
made to PNAP APP-18 and PNAP APP-137 on details of monitoring requirements if
excavation and lateral support (ELS) works, pile installation works and similar operations
are proposed.

Criteria for Submission of ELS Plans

2.             In general terms, ELS plans are required under Building (Administration)
Regulation 8(1)(bc) to be submitted for approval where excavations of substantial depth
are to be carried out, the collapse of which would cause serious consequences.

3.         ELS plans will be required to be submitted to the Buildings Department
(BD) for approval where the excavation works to be carried out are:

          (a)   deeper than 2.5 m and greater than 5 m in length; and

         (b)   liable to affect any road, building, structure, slope steeper than
             30° or water main 75 mm in diameter or greater, the affected area
             being defined as within the 45° line up from the base of the
              excavation to the ground surface.

4.          Where the above conditions apply, consent to commence ELS works
would not be given if ELS plans, as prescribed in Building (Administration) Regulation
8(1)(bc), have not been submitted and approved.

5.          Where ELS plans are required to be submitted for approval as stipulated
above, an RSE would be required to prepare and sign the plans and structural design as
well as the structural assessment report of the effects of the excavation and dewatering
on adjoining structures. For ELS plans with excavation depth deeper than 4.5m, an RGE
would be  required  to  prepare and  sign  the supporting documentations, such  as
geotechnical assessment, geotechnical details and calculations, geotechnical reports, site
investigation reports or ground investigation reports, which should accompany the ELS
plans submission.

6.                  It is permissible to have the ELS plans submitted in two stages, provided
that the first stage submission demonstrates the feasibility of the entire scheme.  Plans
submitted in the first stage should show the pile wall system, for example sheet piles or a
diaphragm wall, together with a strutting layout and the construction sequence.  The
supporting geotechnical documentation should also be provided in the  first stage
submission.  Calculations submitted in the first stage should include realistic ground
movement estimates for the entire works. The second stage submission typically would
include the structural design and details of the lateral support system, including detailing
of the walings and struts for each stage of excavation according to the approved first stage
submission. To minimise the idling time on construction sites, earth-retaining elements
such as sheet/pipe pile walls may be installed and, if applicable, initial stage ELS works1
may be carried out concurrently with the foundation works. For further details, please
refer to Appendix C of PNAP APP-18.

Global Factor Method

7.          The Global Factor Method (GFM) has generally been used in the design
of ELS works. In this method, unfactored soil strength parameters and loads are used in
the stability checks. A minimum global factor of safety of 1.5 should be adopted for
design against overturning/toe stability by using effective stress analysis with drained
shear strength of soil. If total stress analysis with undrained shear strength of soil is used
for design against overturning/toe stability2, a minimum global factor of safety of 2.0
should be adopted.  Guidance and requirements are given in the “Notes on Design of
Excavation and Lateral Support Works” in Appendix A.

Partial Factor Method

8.          With the publication of GEO Publication No. 1/2023 — Deep Excavation
Design and Construction by the Geotechnical Engineering Office of the Civil Engineering
and Development Department in 2023, Partial Factor Method (PFM) could be used, as an
alternative, for analysis and design of ELS works.   In this method, soil structure
interaction analysis is carried out for both the serviceability limit state and ultimate limit
state conditions. Soil parameters and loads should be applied with partial factors in the
design of ELS works. This alternative method should also conform to the guidance and
requirements given in the “Notes on Design of Excavation and Lateral Support Works”
in Appendix A.

Submission Requirements

9.             In all ELS plans submissions, the design method used should be stated on
the plans and calculations. The submission procedures for ELS works adopting either
GFM or PFM will be the same. However, ELS works design based on a mixture of both
methods is not allowed.

Appendix A

           Notes on Design of Excavation and Lateral Support Works

1.     Introduction

1.1    This document provides technical guidance and requirements for the design of
       excavation and lateral support (ELS) works in Hong Kong.  Those given in
       Section 2 are general guidance and requirements for ELS works and are applicable
        to both the Global Factor Method (GFM) and Partial Factor Method (PFM).
      Those given in Section 3 are applicable to the PFM only.

2.    General Guidance and Requirements (APPLICABLE TO BOTH GFM AND
     PFM)

2.1    Serviceability Limit State (SLS) Checks

2.1.1  Where necessary, SLS checks should be carried out to assess the impacts on
       adjoining buildings, structures, services and land.

2.1.2  For the assessment of pile wall deflections and associated ground movements at
        different stages of ELS works including dewatering and excavation, the guidance
       given  in GEO  Publication No. 1/2023 — Deep Excavation Design and
       Construction should be followed.

2.2    Soil Stiffness

2.2.1  In applying soil stiffness from correlations with SPT ‘N’ values based on reliable
       published case histories, account should be taken of the soil type and the computer
      program used in the back analyses for deriving the correlation.

2.3   Base Heave and Hydraulic Failure

2.3.1  The guidance given in Section 6.2.3 and 6.2.4 of GEO Publication No. 1/2023
       should be followed and a minimum factor of safety of 1.5 against base heave and
       hydraulic failure should be used.

2.3.2  In the design, account should be taken of the uncertainties in ground conditions
        (e.g. layered soil with heterogeneity in permeability) and the applicability of the
       design charts for the particular site conditions.

2.4    Design Report and Design Review

2.4.1  The design report should be certified by the RSE and RGE.  It should state that:

        (a)   the analyses demonstrate that there is an adequate safety margin
             against collapse of the excavation, and

(b)   there will be no unacceptable impacts due to the ELS works
            provided that the design assumptions are verified and found to be
             valid  and  the  works  are  carried  out  as  specified  during
             construction.

2.4.2  The design report should indicate the design assumptions required to be reviewed
      and verified during construction, the range of computation results for the key
       parameters  requiring  monitoring  and  checking,  and  the  critical  working
       procedures requiring inspection and certification during construction.

2.4.3 A copy of the design report should be made available to the qualified site
       supervision personnel responsible.

2.4.4  Monitoring should be carried out to review and check the performance of the
       design during construction.  As a minimum, ground settlement/heave and
       piezometric readings should be monitored to provide data for design review, risk
      management/control and performance review. Where there is significant risk to
         life and property in the event of a failure, monitoring of pile wall deflections and
       associated ground movements, water leakage and ground loss through or beneath
       the wall, as well as the change to the flow of groundwater should also be carried
        out.

3.    Guidance and Requirements (APPLICABLE TO PFM ONLY)

3.1   Scope

3.1.1 PFM can be used for ELS works including those with pile walls socketed in rock
       or embedded in soil.

3.2    Design Parameters

3.2.1  The  partial factors and surcharge loads recommended in GEO Publication
      No. 1/2023, together with its guidance on site investigation, ground modelling,
       selection of geotechnical parameters and design water pressures, should be
       adopted except that the partial load factor of 1.5 for surcharge load should be
       adopted instead of 1.3.

3.3    Structural Design

3.3.1 PFM should be used for structural design. A factor of 1.4 should be applied to
       the forces and bending moments obtained from the SLS calculations carried out
        in accordance with the GEO Publication No.1/2023.

3.3.2  Additional structural calculations using the partial factors given in the relevant
        structural design codes may be required to check the structural members.

3.4    Ultimate Limit State (ULS) Checks

3.4.1  ULS calculations should be carried out using limit equilibrium method or soil
        structure interaction analysis to ensure stability with an adequate safety margin.

3.4.2  When ULS checks are based on the recommendations given in this Appendix,
       there  is no need  to carry out separate checks against moment  instability
          (i.e. overturning and kick-out failures) using a global factor of safety of 1.5 (for
        effective stress analysis) or 2.0 (for total stress analysis), as required in GFM.

3.5    Sensitivity Analyses in ULS Checks

3.5.1  Under the GEO Publication No.1/2023, the limit equilibrium method may be used
        to determine the minimum wall embedment depth required to ensure an adequate
       safety margin against moment instability. Alternatively, soil structure interaction
       analyses method may be carried out.

3.5.2  In soil structure interaction analyses, there could be a rapid increase in the
     maximum pile wall deflection/strut loads upon a small reduction in the wall
      embedment depth, reflecting the sensitivity of the design to small variations in
       wall embedment. Hence a suitable value of design wall embedment depth should
      be selected to take into account the results of sensitivity analysis and the
       construction tolerance that can be achieved under the construction control and
       supervision regime imposed.

3.6    Design of ELS Works Socketed in Rock

3.6.1  For the design principles and instability checks of rock sockets, reference should
      be made to PFM for the design of rock-socketed cantilever walls as outlined in
       Chapter 7 of GEO Publication No. 1/2023.
