                         Elderly-friendly Building Design
    Regulation 73 and Fourth Schedule of the Building (Planning) Regulations

Application

          With the coming into operation of the Building (Planning) (Amendment)
Regulation 2026 on 1 August 2026, regulation 73 and Fourth Schedule of the Building
(Planning) Regulations (B(P)R) require a building or a residential unit in a building that
falls within the scope of application1 to be designed to the satisfaction of the Building
Authority (BA) in a manner that will facilitate the access to, and use of, the building or
unit and its facilities by elderly persons.

2.        By virtue of section 39(2) of the Buildings Ordinance (BO), the mandatory
requirements for elderly-friendly building design do not apply to building works which
are being carried out or consent to the commencement of which has been given as at
1 August 2026. In general, building works may refer to foundation works of the proposed
development.

3.        As regards alteration and addition works to an existing building or part of
an existing building falling under the scope of application, the mandatory requirements
for elderly-friendly building design do not apply to building works which are being
carried out or consent to the commencement of works has been given, or minor works
commenced under the Minor Works Control System2 as at 1 August 2026.

4.              If there are special circumstances rendering undue difficulties in complying
with the requirements in existing buildings, exemption from or modification of the
provisions under regulation 73 and Fourth Schedule of the B(P)R may be considered by
the BA based on individual case merits under section 42 of the BO.

Performance Standards

6.           Regulations 72 and 73 of the B(P)R should be read in parallel. While the
Fourth Schedule of the B(P)R stipulates the specific provisions for elderly-friendly design
requirements, the design requirements specified in the Third Schedule of the B(P)R for
persons with a disability shall also apply. For example, section 5 of the Fourth Schedule
of the B(P)R requires provision of dual-level handrails for the main circulation staircase,
other design requirements for handrails, such as dimension, shape and loading, under
Division 8 of the Third Schedule of the B(P)R shall also be complied with.

7.          Performance standards on  slip-resistant  floor  surface and  mirror-like
finishes of accessible lift car, as accepted by the BA for compliance with regulation 73
and Fourth Schedule of the B(P)R, are detailed in Appendices A and B respectively.

Appendix A
                                                 (PNAP APP-175)


                             Slip-resistant Floor Surface


            Floor surfaces required to be slip-resistant shall be tested and classified in
accordance with the following standards, or other equivalent standards:

     (a)      Australian Standard AS 4586:2013, Slip resistance classification of
          new pedestrian surface materials; or

     (b)    German Standard DIN 51130:2014-02, Testing of floor coverings –
            Determination of the anti-slip property – Workrooms and fields of
               activities with slip danger – Walking method – Ramp test.

2.         Under sections 3(2) and 13 of the Fourth Schedule of the B(P)R, floor
surfaces of the regulated routes and wet areas shall attain the respective slip resistance
ratings under the wet pendulum slip test or the oil-wet ramp slip test specified in the table
below1.

               Area               Wet Pendulum    Oil-Wet Ramp
                                                Slip Rating2        Slip Rating3
 Indoor area    regulated route                 P3             R10
               bathroom, kitchen and            P3             R10
                    toilet of a residential unit
 Outdoor area   regulated route                 P4             R11

3.         The classifications generally refer to the minimum standards required for
slip-resistant floor surfaces in different areas.  Higher standards may be adopted
depending on the actual site conditions and operational needs, provided that the risk of
stumbling or tripping resulting from such higher standards has been taken into account.

4.         To maintain optimal slip resistance and enhance safety,  it is crucial to
implement effective drainage systems and other preventive measures.  These efforts
ensure that dry areas remain free from standing or casual water, minimising slip hazards
and promoting a secure environment.

5.          Authorized Persons (AP) are reminded to consider the durability as well as
the ease of routine cleaning, maintenance and repair of the selected flooring materials to
ensure that the floor surfaces will remain slip-resistant throughout its use.

(8/2026)

1  The results from the wet pendulum slip test and the oil-wet ramp slip test, as detailed in the
   classification tables, are distinct and not interchangeable.
2  The wet pendulum slip test, which can be conducted in laboratory or on site, classifies slip
   resistance from P1 to P5, where P5 represents the highest slip resistance rating.  For details,
   please refer to AS 4586:2013.
3  The oil-wet ramp slip test, which is conducted in laboratory, classifies slip resistance from R9
   to R13, where R13 represents the highest slip resistance rating. For details, please refer to
  AS 4586:2013 or DIN 51130:2014-02.

Appendix B
                                                 (PNAP APP-175)


                   Mirror-like Finishes of Accessible Lift Car


           According to section 9(3) of the Fourth Schedule of the B(P)R, the internal
surface facing the entrance of the lift car of an accessible lift shall be provided with
mirror-like finishes in a manner that will assist wheelchair users. Such provision can be
in form of non-breakable mirror or finishes such as mirror-finished stainless steel and
laminating films with a reflective surface, and shall have a clear width not less than
850 mm and its bottom and top edges shall be not more than 300 mm and not less than
1 800 mm above the floor of the lift car respectively.

2.          Decorative designs with non-reflective surfaces within the required area of
the mirror-like finishes are generally acceptable, provided that they do not compromise
its performance on allowing visual feedback for wheelchair users when reversing
backwards into the lift lobby.

3.           Alternative means such as visual display and audio signal achieving the
equivalent performance requirement may also be accepted.  The AP shall submit an
application for exemption from or modification of this provision under section 42 of the
BO with justifications to the BA for consideration.
