                       Occupation of New Buildings
                under Section 21 of the Buildings Ordinance


             This Practice Note outlines the procedures for processing applications for
occupation permits (OP) and sets out measures to facilitate the industry.

Application for an OP

2.            Pursuant to section 21(7) of the Buildings Ordinance (BO), the Building
Authority (BA) shall process an application for an OP within 14 days. As time is of the
essence, full co-operation of the authorized person (AP), registered structural engineer
(RSE),  registered geotechnical engineer (RGE) and  registered  contractor (RC)  is
essential. In addition to ensuring that all building works are satisfactorily completed in
accordance with  the approved  plans and  in  full compliance with  the BO,  the
AP/RSE/RGE should ensure that the requisite certificates, record plans, schedules of
building materials and products, structural and building related documents are submitted
prior to, and in no case later than, the formal application in the specified form (Form
BA13). Applications submitted without the requisite documents may significantly hinder
the processing and result in refusal of the application.

3.         To facilitate the timely processing of an application, the AP/RSE/RGE is
strongly encouraged to submit the requisite documents, such as material testing reports
and product certificates, as soon as they become available rather than waiting until the
OP stage. The use of the Buildings Department’s (BD) Electronic Submission Hub (ESH)
enables the AP/RSE/RGE to make batch submissions and systematically track the
submission progress of the required documents. Details of the submission workflow are
set out in section 2.28 of the ESH Application User Manual.

Joint OP Inspection

4.         Upon receipt of an application for an OP, BD staff will arrange a joint
inspection (OP inspection) with the AP. The AP will, in turn, co-ordinate with the RC
who should take note of any irregularities identified during the inspection for prompt
follow-up action and rectification.

5.           During the OP inspection, all parts of the building works must be available
for examination. If the inspection reveals that any building works shown on the approved
plans are obviously incomplete, e.g. sanitary fitments or fire doors have not been installed,
the application will be summarily refused.  In such cases, a fresh application in the
specified form (Form BA13) should be submitted. In serious cases, consideration will be
given to instigating disciplinary proceedings under the BO for prematurely certifying the
completion of the building works.

6.             Plant and machinery, such as an extensive air conditioning installation,
which are required under the BO to be operational on completion of the building should
be made available for inspection. Generally speaking, provided it is structurally safe to
do so, there is no objection to the installation of plant and fixtures before an OP is issued.
Such installations should not, however, unduly hamper inspection of the completed
building works.

7.                If the OP inspection reveals the need for rectifications, the BA may accept
the AP’s  written  confirmation  and, where  appropriate,  photographic  proof  that
rectification has been made prior to the expiry of the 14-day period. Such confirmation
and proof should, however, be submitted in good time to allow for preparation of the OP.

8.             Notification of refusals of applications will be given via ESH or by email,
fax or post.  In addition to the statutory grounds for refusal, other comments and
observations may be included in the refusal letter for information and any action deemed
appropriate.

9.           Following a refusal, except in the case of a premature application, a formal
resubmission of an application in the specified form (Form BA13) is usually not required.
On receipt of written confirmation from the AP that the refusal items have been rectified
to his satisfaction, the BA will, within 14 days, carry out a reinspection (if required) and
either issue the OP or again refuse the application.

Facilitation Measures

10.       A development project may be extensive in scale and/or complex in
nature.  The following measures are in place  to  facilitate the processing of OP
applications.

Joint Comprehensive Inspection at Critical Stage of Construction

11.        AP/RSE/RGE/RC are responsible for ensuring that the building works are
carried out and completed in accordance with the approved plans, any conditions
imposed, and the BO and its allied regulations. Early identification of non-conformities
can minimise abortive works.  In this connection, BD staff are prepared to conduct a
comprehensive inspection (CI) jointly with the AP at the critical stage of construction
with a view to identifying major non-conformities1 and checking compliance with the
conditions of relevant exemptions/modifications.

12.         The CI should be conducted after completion of the superstructure within
a timeframe the AP considers appropriate to maximise the benefits of early identification
of major non-conformities.  In this connection, the AP should take the initiative to
co-ordinate with BD to fix the schedule for the CI. The CI serves solely as a facilitation
measure to reduce the risk of major non-conformities being identified at a late stage.  It
does not absolve AP/RSE/RGE/RC of their responsibilities to ensure that the building
works are completed in accordance with the approved plans, any conditions imposed, and
the BO and its allied regulations.

Temporary Structural Supports for Scaffolds on External Walls

13.        On certifying completion of the building works, all temporary structures,
such as scaffolds, hoardings, covered walkways and contractor’s sheds, should be
removed to demonstrate that the site is ready for OP inspection and that the development
is  fit for occupation.   Nevertheless, while the use of metal scaffolds has become
increasingly common for building works under construction, their removal often takes
time. To facilitate the final making good works near the time of OP, retaining localised
portions of scaffolds and their temporary structural supports will not render the OP
inspection premature under paragraph 5 above, subject to the following conditions:

        (a)     Prior agreement with BD should be obtained during the CI on the
               extent and locations of such scaffolds and temporary supports to
             be retained at the time of OP application;

        (b)    The localised portions of scaffolds and their temporary structural
              supports should not jeopardise the inspection of the completed
             works;

        (c)    All scaffolds and temporary structural supports must be completely
            removed and the affected areas must be made good before the
              issuance of the OP. Adequate safety precautions should be in place
              during their removal; and

        (d)   A letter from the AP should be submitted after the CI to confirm
               the agreed arrangement, together with a plan clearly demarcating
               the extent and locations of such scaffolds and temporary supports
             and the necessary safety precautions, and that the conditions as
               stipulated in paragraph 13(b) and (c) above will be complied with.

14.              If any of the above-mentioned scaffolds and temporary structural supports
are not removed before expiry of the OP processing period, the OP application will be
refused under section 21(6)(a) of the BO.  Depending on the extent of the final making
good works, a fresh application in the specified form (Form BA13) and a reinspection by
BD may be required.
