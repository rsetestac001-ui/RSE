                  Design Manual on Universal Accessibility


              Regulations 72 and 73, the Third and Fourth Schedules of the Building
(Planning) Regulations (B(P)R) set out the statutory requirements for barrier free access
and facilities (collectively referred to as “barrier free access”) for persons with a disability
and elderly-friendly design in private buildings. The statutory design requirements and
recommended best practices for  barrier free access as well as the recommended
elderly-friendly design guidelines were incorporated in the Design Manual: Barrier Free
Access 2008 (first published in 2008 with the latest edition published in 2025) (DMBFA).

2.           Following the Consultancy Study for Enhancement of the Accessibility of
Physical Environment in Hong Kong commissioned by the Labour and Welfare Bureau,
which recommended the revision of the DMBFA and, where practicable, the promotion
of  universal  design  concepts,  the  Buildings  Department (BD)  commissioned  a
consultancy study in 2024 to undertake a holistic review of the DMBFA, with a view to
enhancing the design standards to align with the latest needs and international trends, and
ensuring that concepts of universal design are incorporated in a way that is feasible and
pragmatic in the local context. The DMBFA has therefore been revamped and expanded
to incorporate universal design concepts with the objective of creating a more inclusive
and accessible built environment.  This culminates in the formulation of the Design
Manual on Universal Accessibility (DMUA), which aims to promote an inclusive built
environment that is accessible and functional for a broader spectrum of the community.

Application

3.           Authorized Persons (AP) and Registered Structural Engineers (RSE)
should refer to regulations 72 and 73, the Third and Fourth Schedules of the B(P)R for
the statutory requirements for barrier free access for persons with a disability and
elderly-friendly design in private buildings.

4.           Save for the provisions already covered by regulations 72 and 73, the Third
and Fourth Schedules of the B(P)R, the requirements under the DMUA are, at present,
advisory in nature. For those obligatory provisions stipulated in the DMUA that are not
yet covered by regulations 72 and 73, the Third and Fourth Schedules of the B(P)R, the
BD  will propose  inclusion  of such  provisions  in  the B(P)R through  legislative
amendments.  In the interim, AP/RSE are encouraged to adopt the DMUA as far as
practicable and to provide feedback on its application to the BD.

Exemption from or Modification of Regulations 72 and 73 of B(P)R

5.             In an application for approval of plans for a new building or for the
alterations and additions to an existing building, barrier free access and elderly-friendly
design in compliance with regulations 72 and 73, the Third and Fourth Schedules of the
B(P)R should be clearly shown on the submitted plans in accordance with regulation 8(1)
of the Building (Administration) Regulations.

6.           There may be occasions where a development proposal or an alteration
and addition proposal cannot fully comply with the requirements stipulated under the
regulations.  Where  it is satisfied that special circumstances exist, applications for
exemption from, or modification of, such requirements will be considered by the Building
Authority (BA) on the individual merits of each case.   Examples of such special
circumstances  include  situations  in  which  the  physical  location  or  immediate
surroundings of a development site render compliance impracticable, the applicant has
no control over the initial access or common parts of an existing building, or where
compliance would impose unjustifiable hardship on the applicant or any other person.

7.          Where alteration and addition works are proposed to an existing building
without barrier free access or elderly-friendly design hence not meeting the requirements
stipulated under the regulations, paragraph 11.6 of the DMUA  sets out  practical
alternatives to guide alteration and addition or improvement works in existing buildings
where spatial or structural constraints are present.

Advisory Committee on Barrier Free Access and Building Committee

8.             In considering applications for exemption from or modification of the
regulations, the BA has established the Advisory Committee on Barrier Free Access
(ACBFA) and the Building Committee (BC) to provide a forum for the deliberation of
proposals related to barrier free access and elderly-friendly design respectively. The BA
will take into account the advice tendered by the ACBFA and the BC when determining
such applications.

Technical Committee on Design Manual on Universal Accessibility

9.          With the revamp and expansion of the design manual to promote universal
design concepts, the extant Technical Committee on Design Manual: Barrier Free Access
will be expanded and renamed as the Technical Committee on Design Manual on
Universal Accessibility to, among others, collect and consider views and feedback from
the building sector, the rehabilitation sector and relevant stakeholder organisations arising
from their practical experience in the use of the DMUA.
