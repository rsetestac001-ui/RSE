Items raised by HKIA 
1.  Amenity Features under PNAP APP-42 
 
The following amenity features under PNAP APP-42 aim to promote elderly 
friendly built environment: 
 
Paragraph 20 – Horizontal Screens / Covered Walkways; 
Paragraph 24 – Shelters for Outdoor Fixed Seats; 
Paragraph 30 – Covered Areas at Public Open Space; 
Paragraph 42 – Covered Picking Up and Setting Down Area for Passengers. 
 
However, paragraph 46 of the PNAP currently stipulates that these features are 
applicable to residential and composite buildings, as well as commercial 
buildings often patronised by the elderly only. 
 
We opine that these amenity features should also be applicable to other 
building types, specifically institutional buildings (e.g. community facilities, 
health care centres and educational facilities). 
 
Generally, designated public and common areas within institutional 
developments are often utilised by elderly persons as well.  The provisions 
of common covered walkways and outdoor sheltered seating also directly 
serve elderly visitors attending day-care services, medical appointments or 
accessing public open space in institutional buildings.   
 
 
BD advised that the amenity features in paragraphs 20, 24, 30 and 42 
under PNAP APP-42 which aim to promote elderly friendly built 
environment were applicable to residential and composite buildings, as 
well as commercial buildings often patronised by the elderly. 
 
For the above amenity features in other types of buildings, such as 
institutional buildings with residential care home for the elderly or day 
care centre which were often patronised by and were genuinely beneficial 
to the elderly, BD would favourably consider exempting such features 
from GFA and/or SC calculations on individual merits.

regarding expected elderly usage and public benefit? 
 
 
Items raised by HKIE 
2.  Wind Load on Canopy with Height Greater Than Two Times The Canopy 
Projection 
 
In Clause B2.3 of Appendix B to the Code of Practice on Wind Effects in 
Hong Kong 2019 (the Wind Code), there is a limit on the height of canopy to 
twice the canopy projection for the adoption of the recommended Cp values. 
Would BD please advise if the Cp values in this clause can be adopted in case 
the height limit is exceeded?  If not, what procedures should be followed in 
assessing the wind load on the canopy? 
 
 
 
 
BD advised that Clause B2.3 of Appendix B to the Wind Code could be 
referred to if the concerned canopy was attached below half-way up the 
building and below a height of twice the canopy projection.   
 
Clause B2.3 of Explanatory Notes to the Wind Code could be referred to 
for canopy attached to upper half of the building or free-standing canopy. 
 
Canopy is defined in regulation 2 under the Building (Planning) 
Regulations that any structure which projects more than 500 mm from any 
wall of any building and at a height of not more than 7.5 m above the level 
of the ground to provide protection from rain or sun, not carrying any 
floor load, either cantilevered or supported by brackets.  In this case, the 
net pressure coefficients could be taken as +0.9 and -1.3 according to 
Clause B2.3 of Appendix B of the Wind Code.

3.  Allowable Lateral Bearing Pressure of Rock With Allowable Vertical 
Bearing Pressure Conforming To The Recommendations in Technical 
Guidance Note No. 53 
 
According to paragraph 6 of Appendix C to PNAP APP-18, allowable vertical 
bearing pressure for Category 1(c) granitic rock may be taken as 7500 kPa 
subject to certain imposed conditions by the BA.  It is specified in Clause 
2.2.2(3) of the Code of Practice for Foundations 2017 (2024 Edition) (the 
Foundation Code) that the allowable lateral bearing pressure of rock may be 
taken as 1/3 of the vertical bearing pressure provided that no adverse rock 
joints exist.  It can thus be interpreted that if 7500 kPa is adopted for the 
allowable vertical bearing pressure of rock, then the allowable lateral bearing 
pressure may be taken as 7500/3 =2500 kPa.  Would BD please advise if the 
above interpretation is correct? 
 
 
 
 
 
 
BD advised that HKIE’s understanding was correct provided that no 
adverse rock joints exist. 
4.  Concurrent Processing of Approval and Consent for Amendment of 
Secondary Structural Element Without First Consent 
 
Regarding an amendment plan for secondary structural elements, for which 
first consent has not been granted, submitted to BA for approval, can the 
concurrent processing of approval and consent as stated in paragraph 34 of 
PNAP ADM-19 be adopted?  Concerns have been raised in some cases that 
as first consent for the works has not been granted, the concurrent processing 
of approval and consent should not apply.  Would BD please advise? 
 
 
 
 
 
BD advised that according to paragraphs 33 and 34 of PNAP ADM-19, 
concurrent application for approval of plans and consent to the 
commencement of secondary structural element works does not limit to 
the first plan submission for these works, provided that the criteria set out 
in paragraph 34(a) to (d) of the PNAP were met.

5.  Arrangement for Rebound Hammer Test 
 
We note that the arrangement for rebound hammer tests for superstructure 
works has recently been changed such that tests are now conducted without 
prior notice.  This has slowed the progress, as preparation of test surfaces 
could not be completed in advance. 
 
Additionally, as the technically competent person (TCP) of grade T3 of RSE’s 
stream was not necessarily stationed full-time at site during the superstructure 
construction period, the requirement for TCP T3 attendance at short notice to 
witness and endorse test results has caused inconvenience to the personnel 
involved. 
 
Would BD please clarify the current arrangement for rebound hammer tests? 
 
 
 
 
BD advised that according to paragraph 3 of PNAP ADM-18 on site 
auditing for building works, BD would conduct surprise checks to audit 
building works without prior notification.  The auditing officer of BD 
would have the discretion to determine the timing of audit inspection 
subject to the nature of various stages of building works on a case-by-case 
basis.

7.  PNAP APP-2 – Aboveground Private/ Public Car Park 
 
According to paragraph 18(b)(i) of PNAP APP-2, GFA for both aboveground 
private car park and aboveground public car park of not more than two floors 
can be 100% disregarded.  Would BD please clarify if the 100% disregarded 
GFA for private car park and public car park are counted separately with not 
more than two floors for each respective use as illustrated in the diagram 
below. 
 
 
 
BD advised that AAP’s diagram indicating the percentage of GFA 
concessions for the aboveground car parks was incorrect.  Paragraph 7 
and its footnote 4 of Appendix C to PNAP APP-2 stated that under 
paragraph 18(b)(i) of this PNAP, not more than two aboveground 
carparking floors (irrespective of the provision of private and public car 
parks) might be 100% disregarded from GFA calculation.

8.  PNAP APP-93 
 
According to paragraph 3(b)(ii) under PNAP APP-93, it is stated that the area 
of pipe wells could be exempted from the GFA calculations and open pipe 
wells may also be exempted from SC calculations.  We understand that pipe 
well is also allowed to be covered provided that the relevant pipe well area is 
included in the SC calculation.  
 
Would BD please advise if the above understanding is correct? 
 
 
 
BD advised that AAP’s understanding was correct, subject to compliance 
with the criteria set in PNAP APP-93, especially the ventilation openings 
with a minimum net area of 1/10th of the horizontal area of the pipe well 
at both the top and bottom of the well.

11. Use of Tied-Back Wall Support System for Excavation Works 
 
Paragraph 10 of the Information Note of LandsD attached to BD’s circular 
letter dated 7 July 2026 states that for ground reinforcements encroaching into 
Government land within a public road, street or footpath or future public road, 
street or footpath maintained or to be maintained by HyD, HyD would be the 
authority to consider and approve the Works. 
 
In this regard, would BD accept the submission of ELS plan for the use of 
temporary ground reinforcements within such Government land, provided that 
these temporary ground reinforcements are installed at a specified depth 
below the existing road/street/footpath level?  Furthermore, if BD does 
accept such ELS scheme, would the workflow set out in Appendix II to the 
Information Note remain applicable?  
 
 
 
BD advised that while BD’s processing of plan submission that involves 
ground reinforcements encroaching into Government land, including 
public roads, streets or footpaths currently maintained by HyD, BD will 
consult the relevant government departments under the centralised 
processing system and collate their comments within time limits allowed 
for processing the plan.  
 
In view of the above, the workflow set out in Appendix II to the LandsD’s 
Information Note on streamlined arrangement for the use of ground 
reinforcements in adjoining Government Land for excavation works 
remains applicable.  Additionally, AP/RSE/RGE might be required to 
apply for modification/exemption to exempt the provisions under section 
31(1) of the BO for the approval of plans.
